What Are AI Applications?

Four categories of capability
The four categories, at a glance

Predictive. Generative.
Agentic. Embedded.

01
Predictive AI
Learns from history to score or forecast the future — a fraud score, a churn risk, a demand forecast.
02
Generative AI
Learns the shape of language or images, then creates new content — text, code, pictures.
03
Agentic AI
Plans, takes actions with tools, and checks its own work — it loops until the goal is actually done.
04
Embedded AI
A small model quietly powering one feature inside a bigger app — no chat window, no separate screen.
AI application type 1 of 4 · Predictive AI

Learns from the past.
Scores the future.

No new content gets created here — just a number. That score feeds a business rule, not a conversation.

AI application type 2 of 4 · Generative AI

Predicts the next token.
One at a time.

There's no lookup happening. The model writes new text, code, or images token by token — shaped by pretraining and optional fine-tuning.

AI application type 3 of 4 · Agentic AI

Plans. Acts.
Checks its own work.

Instead of one answer, the model loops — think, act, observe — calling real tools until the goal is verified as done.

AI application type 4 of 4 · Embedded AI

No chat window.
Just a smarter feature.

The AI is invisible — woven into autocomplete, ranking, or recommendations. Users never know they're talking to a model.

Inside the Vector Database

The memory behind every RAG pipeline
Vector databases · 1 of 6 · Same word, different job

Traditional. Blockchain.
Vector.

Rule of thumb: traditional DB = "give me exactly this." Blockchain = "prove this happened and can't be changed." Vector DB = "give me things like this."

Vector databases · 2 of 6

Exact match
vs. close enough.

"Cheap flights" and "affordable airfare deals" mean the same thing — only one of these databases knows that.

Vector databases · 3 of 6

Every sentence becomes
a point in space.

Embeddings turn meaning into numbers. Similar ideas land close together — regardless of the exact words used.

Vector databases · 4 of 6 · Offline

Load the documents
once, ahead of time.

Chunk, embed, store — this happens once, before any user ever asks a question.

Vector databases · 5 of 6 · Online

Every question,
answered live.

The question gets embedded too, matched against stored chunks, and handed to the LLM alongside the retrieved passages — grounding the answer in your own data.

Vector databases · 6 of 6 · Not just retrieval

One database.
Two very different jobs.

Collection 1
Knowledge base (RAG)
Holds: your docs, wikis, tickets, FAQs
Queried: once per question, same content for every user
Changes: mostly static — re-ingested on a schedule
Answers: "what does our refund policy say?"
Collection 2
Agent memory / context
Holds: past turns, user facts, preferences, prior tool results
Queried: scoped to one user or session, different per person
Changes: written to on every turn — grows as it's used
Answers: "what did this user tell me last week?"

Same embeddings, same similarity search — just a different collection. This is how an agent "remembers" you across sessions without stuffing the entire history into every prompt.

Reality check · Not every category needs every piece

Same gateway.
Very different depth.

Category API Gateway AI Service Layer RAG + Vector DB LLM
Predictive AI Yes Rarely No No
Generative AI Yes Yes Sometimes Yes
Agentic AI Yes Yes Sometimes Yes
Embedded AI Sometimes Rarely Rarely Rarely

Predictive and embedded AI usually skip the LLM, RAG, and vector DB entirely — a trained model plus an inference call is the whole stack. Generative and agentic AI reach for RAG only when grounding answers in your own data, not by default.

Bringing it together

Same gateway.
Different depth.

Every AI application — predictive, generative, agentic, or embedded — shares the same entry point: the API gateway and the AI service layer. What happens after that depends entirely on the category. RAG and vector databases are a tool for grounding, not a universal requirement.
